
\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Quantized retrieval.} Product Quantization compresses vectors for ANN search \citep{jegou2011pq}; random-hyperplane hashing connects sign agreement to angle \citep{charikar2002simhash}. RaBitQ provides randomized low-bit distance estimation with error bounds \citep{gao2024rabitq}. MUVERA compiles multi-vector similarity into fixed-dimensional encodings for MIPS \citep{dhulipala2024muvera}. Our setting differs in the object being compressed: a catalog may serve many reusable semantic functions, so predicate-program storage and multi-predicate execution matter in addition to item-code size. The PQ64 compiled-linear baseline is consequently important rather than ancillary.

\paragraph{Filtered vector search.} Filtered ANN combines vector similarity with structured predicates and introduces nontrivial recall/latency trade-offs depending on selectivity and correlation \citep{chronis2025filtered}. Our final HNSW experiment moves learned soft predicates into this systems setting. The distinction is operational: a structured filter is normally materialized catalog state, whereas our eligibility test is computed from a 56-byte shared item code and a 216-byte independently deployable learned program. Dense similarity continues to navigate the graph.

\paragraph{Semantic operators and proxy execution.} LOTUS formalizes natural-language semantic operators and their optimization \citep{patel2025lotus}. Recent semantic-filter work studies learned proxies and adaptive cascades to reduce expensive oracle calls \citep{kim2026semanticfilter}. Compilation-based semantic execution similarly moves semantic interpretation out of per-item runtime \citep{dong2026compilation}. The design studied here occupies a lower-level systems point: one shared low-bit item substrate, independently compiled semantic predicates, calibrated composition, and direct execution inside a retrieval loop.

\section{What the Experiments Support}
The experiments narrow the claim substantially relative to the initial \rsa hypothesis. First, random orthogonal rotation is not required for the strongest compact result: centered 1-bit identity codes support the best tiny-program design, and the 1-bit sparse identity/random ablation is effectively tied. Second, four-bit sparse LUTs are useful but not Pareto-optimal in the final benchmark. Third, the calibrated log-probability product is operationally useful but should be viewed as a simple composition rule rather than a new algebraic theorem.

The more distinctive result is \emph{joint item--program compression plus live search execution}. Binary1-LS2-int4 uses 56 bytes/item and about 216 bytes of persistent program state while recovering 83\% of the FP32 semantic gain over dense truncation. PQ64 is the stronger compressed quality point, but its memory trade-off must be stated fairly: the 65.5\,KB lookup table used by the native executor can be materialized only for active predicates, while persistent storage can remain a 1.55\,KB linear head per concept plus a shared codebook. Under that accounting, the illustrative 5M-item/100k-concept payload is about 302\,MB for Binary1-LS2-int4 versus about 475\,MB for PQ64, not multiple gigabytes of mandatory PQ program state.

The reviewer-controlled HNSW result adds a second axis. Using 1,000 queries for each of three predetermined three-predicate sets, live execution reaches 0.955--0.992 Traversal Recall@50 from 50\% down to 2\% eligibility. A selectivity-aware over-fetch sweep through $2\times K/s$ does not match live recall within 0.005 in any of the 15 conditions. At 50--10\% eligibility the largest completed over-fetch point is faster but substantially lower recall; at 5\% and 2\% live traversal is both faster and higher recall. The incremental semantic-program cost is approximately 96--134\,ns per predicate invocation, or about 0.16--0.22 of the scalar 384-D dot-product cost measured on the same CPU.

This does \emph{not} mean that semantic scores should steer ANN geometry. Our direct semantic-priority experiment reduced traversal work but damaged recall, while explicit two-hop bridge expansion added work without a meaningful quality gain. The supported algorithm is simpler: dense navigation, soft semantic eligibility, invalid nodes still traversable. It also does not yet establish a universal matched-recall speedup over post-filtering: the completed over-fetch range failed to reach the live operating point, and the public harness now extends the sweep to larger budgets specifically to locate that frontier.

\section{Limitations and Next Experiments}
The main semantic-quality benchmark still covers one fashion catalog, one retrieval encoder, and eight CLIP-defined latent concepts. Teacher labels come from prompt-based CLIP image comparisons rather than human relevance judgments, and concept prevalence is standardized to 40\% on each fit split. The 200 queries per split are synthetically generated from a fixed latent vocabulary and fit-supported exact filters; they are useful for controlled evaluation but are not a natural search-log distribution.

The candidate-budget recall numbers remain conditional on the exact-filtered top-5,000 MiniLM pool. They measure how well a cheap semantic executor allocates a downstream budget, not end-to-end catalog recall. The HNSW experiment addresses execution inside ANN traversal, but it still uses the first strict held-out split and synthetic selectivity thresholds over three active predicates per query. Traversal recall is defined relative to the same compiled predicate, so it evaluates ANN execution fidelity rather than semantic truth.

The Binary1-LS2-int4 compiler is \emph{BBQ-inspired}, not an exact Lucene/Elasticsearch BBQ implementation, so the paper should not be read as a direct benchmark against that production kernel. Likewise, the older \code{hnsw\_rs} filtered-search timing is retained only as library context; the paper no longer attributes the custom-vs-library latency gap to the compiled predicate mechanism. The controlled comparison that matters is live traversal versus selectivity-aware over-fetch on the same graph and the live-versus-materialized custom traversal used to isolate incremental predicate cost.

The Rust implementation is single-threaded and resident-memory. It does not yet measure p99 under concurrency, mmap/segment behavior, distributed RPC, updates/tombstones, or a production graph layout. The 100k-concept memory table is representation arithmetic; the current quality benchmark learns only eight concepts, so large-vocabulary cache behavior remains unmeasured. The current graph contains only the strict held-out split rather than a multi-million-item production-scale catalog.

The highest-value next experiments are therefore: completing the extended matched-recall over-fetch sweep; scaling the resident graph to millions of items and reporting QPS/core; a second dataset/domain; human or stronger held-out semantic judgments; natural LLM-generated search plans; and stores of $10^2$--$10^6$ concepts. A representation-learning direction is to make semantic predicates more locally linear---for example, cluster-conditional linear heads or a small geometry-preserving transform trained for semantic separability---and then test how much of the resulting teacher improvement survives compilation into the same low-bit substrate.

\section{Conclusion}
The study began with a random 4-bit semantic substrate and sparse LUT programs, but the final experiments point to a broader conclusion. Semantic supervision can be compiled offline into reusable search-time predicates over a shared low-bit item representation, and both sides of that interface---item code and predicate program---matter. On the 44,072-product independent-teacher benchmark, Binary1-LS2-int4 reaches 0.337 recall at 20\% retention versus 0.207 for dense truncation, 0.352 for PQ64, and 0.363 for the FP32 linear proxy. It uses 56 bytes/item and about 216 bytes/concept and recovers 83\% of the FP32 incremental recall over dense retrieval.

The systems result goes beyond candidate reranking. Across three predetermined predicate sets and 1,000 queries per set, live compiled predicates maintain approximately 0.96--0.99 Traversal Recall@50 from 50\% down to 2\% eligibility. No selectivity-aware over-fetch point up to $2\times K/s$ reaches that recall within 0.005 in the completed sweep, and the compiled predicate adds only about 96--134\,ns per invocation. Exact live/materialized ID parity is used only as a correctness check.

The strongest supported thesis is therefore not that randomness, four-bit quantization, or a particular conjunction operator is uniquely novel. It is that \emph{compiled semantic predicates can become first-class search artifacts}: tiny learned programs over one shared item substrate, selected by query understanding, composed when needed, and executed cheaply enough to participate directly in ANN traversal.

\section*{Impact Statement}
If successful at scale, compiled semantic predicates could reduce online model inference and energy use in retrieval systems while enabling richer natural-language constraints. The same mechanism could also encode biased or poorly specified teacher judgments at scale; deployed systems should therefore audit predicate labels, calibration, subgroup behavior, and failure modes, and should preserve conventional safety and policy filters outside the learned semantic layer.
