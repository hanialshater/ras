
\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Quantized retrieval.} Product Quantization compresses vectors for ANN search \citep{jegou2011pq}; random-hyperplane hashing connects sign agreement to angle \citep{charikar2002simhash}. RaBitQ provides randomized low-bit distance estimation with error bounds \citep{gao2024rabitq}. MUVERA compiles multi-vector similarity into fixed-dimensional encodings for MIPS \citep{dhulipala2024muvera}. Our setting differs in the object being compressed: a catalog may serve many reusable semantic functions, so predicate-program storage and multi-predicate execution matter in addition to item-code size. The PQ64 compiled-linear baseline is consequently important rather than merely ancillary.

\paragraph{Filtered vector search.} Filtered ANN combines vector similarity with structured predicates and introduces nontrivial recall/latency trade-offs depending on selectivity and correlation \citep{chronis2025filtered}. Our final HNSW experiment moves learned soft predicates into this systems setting. The distinction is operational: a structured filter is normally materialized catalog state, whereas our eligibility test is computed from a 56-byte shared item code and a 216-byte independently deployable learned program. Dense similarity continues to navigate the graph.

\paragraph{Semantic operators and proxy execution.} LOTUS formalizes natural-language semantic operators and their optimization \citep{patel2025lotus}. Recent semantic-filter work studies learned proxies and adaptive cascades to reduce expensive oracle calls \citep{kim2026semanticfilter}. Compilation-based semantic execution similarly moves semantic interpretation out of per-item runtime \citep{dong2026compilation}. The design studied here occupies a lower-level systems point: one shared low-bit item substrate, independently compiled semantic predicates, calibrated composition, and direct execution inside a retrieval loop.

\section{What the Experiments Support}
The experiments narrow the claim substantially relative to the initial \rsa hypothesis. First, random orthogonal rotation is not required for the strongest compact result: centered 1-bit identity codes support the best tiny-program design, and the 1-bit sparse identity/random ablation is effectively tied. Second, four-bit sparse LUTs are useful but not Pareto-optimal in the final benchmark. Third, the calibrated log-probability product is operationally useful but should be viewed as a simple composition rule rather than a new algebraic theorem.

The more distinctive result is \emph{joint item--program compression plus live search execution}. PQ64 nearly preserves the FP32 linear semantic proxy, but compiling one linear predicate over all 64 PQ subspaces produces about 65.5\,KB of LUT state. Binary1-LS2-int4 uses 56 bytes/item and about 216 bytes/concept. At five million items and 100k concepts, the representation arithmetic is about 302\,MB for Binary1-LS2-int4 versus 6.87\,GB for PQ64 before generic container and graph overhead.

The HNSW result adds a second axis. The compiled predicate does not need to be applied only after retrieval. When dense geometry performs graph navigation and the learned predicate controls result eligibility, post-filter recall collapse can be avoided without materializing a bitmap for every soft concept. Across 50\% to 2\% eligibility, live Binary1-LS2-int4 maintains roughly 0.97--0.98 Recall@50 and uses 43--52\% of the mean latency of the same-geometry materialized filtered-HNSW baseline in our Rust prototype. The measured semantic-program cost is roughly 108--114\,ns per predicate invocation.

This does \emph{not} mean that semantic scores should steer ANN geometry. Our direct semantic-priority experiment reduced traversal work but damaged recall, while explicit two-hop bridge expansion added work without a meaningful quality gain. The supported algorithm is simpler: dense navigation, soft semantic eligibility, invalid nodes still traversable.

\section{Limitations and Next Experiments}
The main semantic-quality benchmark still covers one fashion catalog, one retrieval encoder, and eight CLIP-defined latent concepts. Teacher labels come from prompt-based CLIP image comparisons rather than human relevance judgments, and concept prevalence is standardized to 40\% on each fit split. The 200 queries per split are synthetically generated from a fixed latent vocabulary and fit-supported exact filters; they are useful for controlled evaluation but are not a natural search-log distribution.

The candidate-budget recall numbers remain conditional on the exact-filtered top-5,000 MiniLM pool. They measure how well a cheap semantic executor allocates a downstream budget, not end-to-end catalog recall. The newer HNSW experiment addresses execution inside ANN traversal, but it uses 100 deterministic query items on the first strict held-out split and synthetic selectivity thresholds over three active predicates. It is therefore a controlled systems benchmark rather than an application-level relevance study.

The Binary1-LS2-int4 compiler is \emph{BBQ-inspired}, not an exact Lucene/Elasticsearch BBQ implementation, so the paper should not be read as a direct benchmark against that production kernel. Likewise, the filtered-HNSW comparison is against the \code{hnsw\_rs} implementation on the same normalized-dot graph in one Rust prototype. The 43--52\% latency ratio should not be generalized to every production filtered-vector engine. Its role is to demonstrate that the compiled predicate cost is small enough to live inside traversal while preserving recall in this controlled setting.

The Rust implementation is single-threaded and resident-memory. It does not yet measure p99 under concurrency, mmap/segment behavior, distributed RPC, updates/tombstones, or a production graph layout. The 100k-concept memory table is representation arithmetic; the current quality benchmark learns only eight concepts, so large-vocabulary cache behavior remains unmeasured.

The highest-value next experiments are therefore external-validity and deployment tests rather than more traversal variants: a second dataset/domain; human or stronger held-out judgments; natural LLM-generated search plans; stores of $10^2$--$10^6$ concepts; and integration with a production-grade ANN implementation. A theoretical direction is to characterize the joint rate--distortion problem for a family of semantic functions rather than only reconstructing item vectors.

\section{Conclusion}
The study began with a random 4-bit semantic substrate and sparse LUT programs, but the final experiments point to a broader conclusion. Semantic supervision can be compiled offline into reusable search-time predicates over a shared low-bit item representation, and both sides of that interface---item code and predicate program---matter. On the 44,072-product independent-teacher benchmark, Binary1-LS2-int4 reaches 0.337 recall at 20\% retention versus 0.207 for dense truncation, 0.352 for PQ64, and 0.363 for the FP32 linear proxy. It uses 56 bytes/item and about 216 bytes/concept and recovers 83\% of the FP32 incremental recall over dense retrieval.

The systems result goes beyond candidate reranking. On the first full-data held-out HNSW benchmark, live compiled predicates preserve approximately 0.97--0.98 Recall@50 from 50\% down to 2\% eligibility, exactly match the corresponding materialized custom traversal, and cost about 110\,ns per predicate invocation. In the same prototype they use roughly half the mean latency of the same-geometry materialized filtered-HNSW baseline while post-filtering loses most relevant results at low selectivity.

The strongest supported thesis is therefore not that randomness, four-bit quantization, or a particular conjunction operator is uniquely novel. It is that \emph{compiled semantic predicates can become first-class search artifacts}: tiny learned programs over one shared item substrate, selected by query understanding, composed when needed, and executed cheaply enough to participate directly in ANN traversal.

\section*{Impact Statement}
If successful at scale, compiled semantic predicates could reduce online model inference and energy use in retrieval systems while enabling richer natural-language constraints. The same mechanism could also encode biased or poorly specified teacher judgments at scale; deployed systems should therefore audit predicate labels, calibration, subgroup behavior, and failure modes, and should preserve conventional safety and policy filters outside the learned semantic layer.
