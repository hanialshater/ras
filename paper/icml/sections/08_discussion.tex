
\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Quantized retrieval.} Product Quantization compresses vectors for ANN search \citep{jegou2011pq}; random-hyperplane hashing connects sign agreement to angle \citep{charikar2002simhash}. RaBitQ provides randomized low-bit distance estimation with error bounds \citep{gao2024rabitq}. MUVERA compiles multi-vector similarity into fixed-dimensional encodings for MIPS \citep{dhulipala2024muvera}. Our setting differs in the object being compressed: a catalog may serve many reusable semantic functions, so predicate-program storage and multi-predicate execution matter in addition to item-code size. The PQ64 compiled-linear baseline is consequently important rather than merely ancillary.

\paragraph{Filtered vector search.} Filtered ANN combines vector similarity with structured predicates and introduces nontrivial recall/latency trade-offs depending on selectivity and correlation \citep{chronis2025filtered}. Our semantic executor is complementary: exact structured predicates should remain native filters; the learned layer targets semantic judgments that are not explicit fields.

\paragraph{Semantic operators and proxy execution.} LOTUS formalizes natural-language semantic operators and their optimization \citep{patel2025lotus}. Recent semantic-filter work studies learned proxies and adaptive cascades to reduce expensive oracle calls \citep{kim2026semanticfilter}. Compilation-based semantic execution similarly moves semantic interpretation out of per-item runtime \citep{dong2026compilation}. The design studied here occupies a low-level systems point: one shared low-bit item substrate, independently compiled semantic predicates, and cheap calibrated composition inside a candidate-pruning stage.

\section{What the Experiments Support}
The experiments narrow the claim substantially relative to the initial \rsa hypothesis. First, random orthogonal rotation is not required for the strongest compact result: centered 1-bit identity codes support the best tiny-program design, and the 1-bit sparse identity/random ablation is effectively tied. Second, four-bit sparse LUTs are useful but not Pareto-optimal in the final benchmark. Third, the calibrated log-probability product is operationally useful but should be viewed as a simple composition rule rather than a new algebraic theorem.

The most distinctive empirical trade-off is instead \emph{joint item--program compression}. PQ64 is a strong item representation and nearly preserves the FP32 linear semantic proxy, but compiling one linear predicate over all 64 PQ subspaces produces about 65.5\,KB of LUT state. Binary1-LS2-int4 uses 56 bytes/item and about 216 bytes/concept. Equation~\eqref{eq:joint_memory} makes this difference explicit. For illustration, at five million items and ten thousand compiled concepts, the benchmark accounting gives approximately 975\,MB for PQ64 versus 282\,MB for Binary1-LS2-int4, before generic container overhead. This example is a storage calculation, not a latency measurement for a ten-thousand-concept serving system.

\section{Limitations and Next Experiments}
The main benchmark still covers one fashion catalog, one retrieval encoder, and eight CLIP-defined latent concepts. Teacher labels come from prompt-based CLIP image comparisons rather than human relevance judgments, and concept prevalence is standardized to 40\% on each fit split. The 200 queries per split are synthetically generated from a fixed latent vocabulary and fit-supported exact filters; they are useful for controlled evaluation but are not a natural search-log distribution.

Recall is conditional on the exact-filtered top-5,000 MiniLM candidate pool. It measures how well a cheap semantic executor allocates a downstream candidate budget, not end-to-end catalog recall. The oracle therefore upper-bounds reranking within that pool rather than retrieval from the entire catalog. The query-level bootstrap also treats overlapping query--split cases more independently than a deployment setting would justify.

The binary compiler is \emph{BBQ-inspired}, not an exact Lucene/Elasticsearch BBQ implementation, so the paper should not be read as a direct benchmark against that production kernel. The native results are from a single-threaded Rust prototype over real learned codes and programs, with the source held-out rows physically tiled to create a 500k-item resident working set. The CPU model was not recorded, there is no hand-written SIMD kernel, and the benchmark does not include ANN traversal, filtering, top-$k$ selection, or downstream ranking. It also activates at most eight concepts and therefore does not directly demonstrate the cache behavior implied by the 303$\times$ program-size gap.

The highest-value next experiments are: (i) exact or library-backed production quantization baselines; (ii) a second dataset/domain and human or stronger held-out semantic judgments; (iii) end-to-end ANN integration reporting global recall/NDCG and latency; and (iv) a large predicate-vocabulary benchmark that separately stresses item memory, program-store memory, and shared execution. A theoretical direction is to characterize the joint rate--distortion problem for a family of semantic functions rather than only reconstructing item vectors.

\section{Conclusion}
The study began with a random 4-bit semantic substrate and sparse LUT programs, but the final experiments point to a broader conclusion. Semantic supervision can be compiled offline into reusable search-time predicates over a shared low-bit item representation, and both sides of that interface---item code and predicate program---must be measured. On 44,072 products and 600 compound query--split cases, Binary1-LS2-int4 reaches 0.337 recall at 20\% retention versus 0.207 for dense truncation, 0.352 for PQ64, and 0.363 for the FP32 linear proxy. It uses 56 bytes/item and about 216 bytes/concept, recovers 83\% of the FP32 incremental recall over dense retrieval, and executes eight real learned predicates at 1.98M candidates/s in the native prototype, close to PQ64's 2.04M/s and about 4.8$\times$ FP32.

The strongest supported thesis is therefore not that randomness, four-bit quantization, or a particular conjunction operator is uniquely novel. It is that \emph{compiled semantic predicate execution} can occupy a useful systems regime between dense similarity and exact filtering: expensive judgments are learned offline, represented as tiny reusable programs, composed at query time, and executed cheaply before the expensive ranker.

\section*{Impact Statement}
If successful at scale, compiled semantic predicates could reduce online model inference and energy use in retrieval systems while enabling richer natural-language constraints. The same mechanism could also encode biased or poorly specified teacher judgments at scale; deployed systems should therefore audit predicate labels, calibration, subgroup behavior, and failure modes, and should preserve conventional safety and policy filters outside the learned semantic layer.
