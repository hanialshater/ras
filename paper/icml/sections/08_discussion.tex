\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Quantized retrieval.} Product Quantization compresses vectors for ANN search \citep{jegou2011pq}; random-hyperplane hashing connects sign agreement to angle \citep{charikar2002simhash}. RaBitQ provides randomized low-bit distance estimation with error bounds \citep{gao2024rabitq}. MUVERA compiles multi-vector similarity into fixed-dimensional encodings for MIPS \citep{dhulipala2024muvera}. \rsa shares this compilation philosophy but targets reusable semantic predicates over a shared item code.

\paragraph{Filtered vector search.} Filtered ANN combines vector similarity with structured predicates and introduces nontrivial recall/latency trade-offs depending on selectivity and correlation \citep{chronis2025filtered}. \rsa is complementary: exact structured predicates should remain native filters; \rsa targets semantic judgments that are not explicit fields.

\paragraph{Semantic operators and proxy execution.} LOTUS formalizes natural-language semantic operators and their optimization \citep{patel2025lotus}. Recent semantic-filter work studies learned proxies and adaptive cascades to reduce expensive oracle calls \citep{kim2026semanticfilter}. Compilation-based semantic execution similarly moves semantic interpretation out of per-item runtime \citep{dong2026compilation}. \rsa occupies a distinct low-level design point: a fixed low-bit item substrate, sparse learned LUT programs, and explicit algebraic composition for retrieval-time execution.

\section{Limitations and Next Experiments}
The main benchmark is substantially larger than the initial pilot, but it still covers one fashion catalog, one retrieval encoder, and eight CLIP-defined latent concepts. Teacher labels come from prompt-based CLIP image comparisons rather than human relevance judgments, and concept prevalence is deliberately standardized to 40\% on each fit split. The 200 queries per split are synthetically generated from a fixed latent vocabulary and fit-supported exact filters; they are useful for controlled evaluation but are not a natural search-log distribution.

Our recall metric is conditional on the exact-filtered top-5,000 MiniLM candidate pool. It measures how well a cheap semantic executor allocates a downstream candidate budget, not end-to-end catalog recall. The oracle therefore upper-bounds reranking within that pool rather than retrieval from the entire catalog. The query-level bootstrap also treats overlapping query--split cases more independently than a deployment setting would justify; the strong seed consistency is reassuring, but a hierarchical benchmark across datasets and query sources would be more definitive.

Finally, the prototype does not yet establish the systems claim experimentally. Codes are represented in Python/NumPy rather than a packed nibble layout, and we have not measured a cache-aware int8/SIMD implementation against optimized linear or neural proxies. The highest-value next experiments are therefore: a second dataset/domain, human or stronger VLM judgments for a held-out query set, direct latency/throughput measurements of packed \rsa execution, and end-to-end ANN integration reporting global recall/NDCG versus latency and retained candidates.

\section{Conclusion}
\rsa explores a semantic-search primitive between similarity and exact filtering. A fixed 4-bit item code can host sparse compiled predicates; residual boosting captures substantially more signal than hard boxes or independent likelihood factors; and calibration enables useful AND/NOT composition without conjunction-specific training. On 44,072 products and 600 independently generated query--split cases, \rsa moves the candidate-budget frontier well beyond dense-only truncation while retaining most of the incremental benefit of a full-precision semantic proxy. At 20\% retention, recall improves from 0.207 to 0.330 and purity from 0.425 to 0.571, with similar gains across three strict splits. The remaining gap to the teacher oracle is dominated by semantic information unavailable to the title representation rather than by low-bit execution alone. These results motivate a concrete systems direction: move expensive semantic supervision offline and execute reusable semantic decisions as tiny, composable search-time programs.

\section*{Impact Statement}
If successful at scale, compiled semantic predicates could reduce online model inference and energy use in retrieval systems while enabling richer natural-language constraints. The same mechanism could also encode biased or poorly specified teacher judgments at scale; deployed systems should therefore audit predicate labels, calibration, subgroup behavior, and failure modes, and should preserve conventional safety and policy filters outside the learned semantic layer.
