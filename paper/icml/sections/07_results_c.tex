\subsection{Large-scale fixed-budget candidate pruning}
Figure~\ref{fig:large_retention} and Table~\ref{tab:large_retention} summarize the main 44,072-product benchmark. \rsa dominates dense-only truncation at every nontrivial candidate budget. At 20\% retention, mean recall rises from 0.207 to 0.330, a 0.123 absolute gain (95\% paired bootstrap interval $[0.112,0.134]$) and a 59\% relative improvement. Purity rises simultaneously from 0.425 to 0.571, a paired gain of 0.145 $[0.135,0.156]$.

\begin{equation}
\frac{0.330-0.207}{0.363-0.207}=0.791

of the additional recall available from the full-precision text-side semantic model. This fraction increases to 0.813 at 40\% retention. \rsa also beats dense recall on 86.7\% of query--split cases at 20\% retention and 88.2\% at 40\%.

The effect is stable across the three strict splits. At 20\%, the mean RSA-minus-dense recall gains are $+0.1244$, $+0.1261$, and $+0.1179$ for seeds 7, 17, and 27 respectively.

\subsection{Gains grow with semantic composition depth}
The benchmark generator creates one to three positive latent predicates and optionally one negated predicate. Table~\ref{tab:complexity} stratifies the 20\% candidate-budget result by the number of latent predicates in the query. Dense recall remains roughly flat, while \rsa's advantage increases as more independently compiled semantic constraints must be satisfied.
